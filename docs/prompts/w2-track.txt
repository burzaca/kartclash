The track module must be PURE (no THREE import, no DOM) — it answers queries only:
sample / halfWidth / surfaceY / progressOf / checkpoints / gridSlot / itemNodes / pads / decor /
bounds / minimap, with the exact return shapes shown in section 1. sample() is called about fifty times
per frame by physics and items, so use the hint index (nearestOnPolyline with a window) and keep it
free of allocation-heavy work where you can.

Elevation: surfaceY comes from def.elev (amp, waves, phase) sampled by lap fraction — smooth, gentle,
and consistent: a kart resting on the surface at any x,z must get exactly surfaceY(x,z). slopeX/slopeZ
by finite difference. The renderer lifts the road ribbon by surfaceY so the road and the surface query
never disagree — that is important, a mismatch would make karts hover or sink.

The mesh side is where the game looks like a real kart racer, so make it look designed:
road ribbon with vertex colours (asphalt with a subtly darker racing line, a white-and-black checkered
start/finish band, glowing chevron boost pads), red/white kerb blocks along the corner edges, a solid
low barrier with posts along both edges (it must read as a wall you bounce off), grass ground with
mottled vertex colour, and the props from track.decor as InstancedMesh groups (one per kind) using
instanceColor: trees (trunk + a lathed or icosahedron canopy), rocks, bushes, cone stacks, tyre
stacks, lamp posts, flag banners on poles, a grandstand with rows of seats near the start, a start/
finish gantry arch over the road, and a few sponsor billboards. No flatShading anywhere. No texture
files, no external assets. Segment counts high enough that nothing looks like a toy block.
